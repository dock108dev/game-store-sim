## What To Ignore

The screenshots are taken from videos and streams. The following are reference noise, not game targets:

- Facecams.
- Chat overlays.
- Video controls.
- YouTube/Twitch title bars.
- Creator watermarks.
- Sponsor panels or stream alerts.

## Useful Visual Patterns

Store exterior:

- Strip-mall frontage with large glass windows and readable sign.
- Neon or lit signage that can be customized.
- Sidewalk approach that makes the shop feel like a place rather than a menu.
- Morning opening time visible in the HUD in several references.

Interior:

- Counter as the command center.
- Office/backroom as the place for catalogue, computer, ordering, and suspicious paperwork.
- Shelves and display cases placed manually in a small rectangular footprint.
- Bright wall color and flooring changes used to make the store feel personalized.
- Arcade, concessions, TV, posters, signs, and novelty equipment as store personality.

UI and interaction:

- Object placement with ghost preview, valid/invalid placement colors, rotation, and grid/snap hints.
- Catalogue-style purchasing interface with categories for fixtures, decorations, equipment, and stations.
- Storefront sign customization with name, style, and color controls.
- Price-setting modal that shows unit cost, market price, sale price, and profit.
- Customer sale negotiation with ask price, market price, player offer, accept/decline/think actions.
- Product/card inspection with grade, rarity, value, and collection context.
- Register checkout with itemized cart, total, customer payment, and change due.

## Useful Mechanical Patterns

Store setup:

- Buy fixtures from a catalogue.
- Place shelves, displays, counters, and service stations.
- Change walls, floors, lights, and signage.
- Expand from a sparse starter store into a dense retail floor.

Stock handling:

- Order supplies online or through a computer/catalogue.
- Receive boxes.
- Open boxes manually.
- Sort products into store inventory or direct shelf stock.
- Assign product slots to fixtures.
- Place units on shelves.
- Price items relative to market value and margin goals.

Customer interaction:

- Customers enter with a purpose: browse, buy, trade, sell, preorder, complain, return, ask for help, or loiter.
- The player answers at the counter or approaches on the floor.
- Transactions can be routine or negotiated.
- Customers react to price, wait time, store reputation, and product availability.

Economy:

- Every item needs at least cost, market price, target price, condition, category, and demand.
- Profit is understandable at the point of decision.
- Overpricing should move slowly and reduce reputation.
- Underpricing should clear stock quickly but create cash-flow pressure.

## Specific Ideas For Mallcore Sim

- Used game trade-ins should be the signature version of card appraisal.
- The player should inspect condition: scratched disc, cracked case, missing manual, fake cartridge, resealed box, smoke smell, water damage.
- Store categories should include used games, new releases, retro games, consoles, controllers, memory cards, strategy guides, trading cards, snacks, repairs, and event tickets.
- Preorders should create future obligations. Missing a launch allocation should hurt reputation.
- Staff picks and endcaps should shape demand.
- The backroom should be both practical and narrative: receiving, storage, repairs, paperwork, safe, security footage, and suspicious packages.
- Store fixtures should have tradeoffs: peg wall, glass case, bargain bin, locked cabinet, demo kiosk, counter rack, console wall, new-release wall.

## Visual Direction

Start with stylized realism rather than hyperrealism:

- Readable silhouettes for every product and fixture.
- Slightly exaggerated signage and UI so price, category, and interaction state are legible.
- Warm fluorescent retail lighting with neon accents.
- Mall-era clutter, but not so much that shelf state becomes unreadable.
- Fictional brand names that evoke the era without legal dependency.
