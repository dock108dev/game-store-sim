## Screenshot Teardown Board

### `main_scene.png`

Current read:

- Validated mechanics are present, but the view reads as a graybox room.
- Ceiling and large flat planes dominate the composition.
- The counter and signage are cropped, so the store brand is not a confident first-viewport signal.
- Product, customer, backroom, and register surfaces exist but feel scattered across a test scene.
- The back wall does not yet communicate a specialty game shop.

Production target:

- First view should immediately read as `SAVE POINT GAMES`, a small used-game shop in operation.
- The player spawn should frame the counter, primary shelf wall, customer lane, and backroom/receiving hint.
- Ceiling should not dominate the shot; the player view should feel human-scaled.
- At least three product/category signals should be visible without inspecting: used games, new releases/preorders, trade-ins/services.
- The player should know where to go first from world composition, not from docs.

### `register_counter.png`

Current read:

- Register exists and validates, but the counter is a large block with clipped signage.
- The register monitor is oversized and hides the work surface.
- The counter does not yet show scanner, payment, bagging, impulse, receipt, or customer-facing details.
- Backroom props appear behind it, but the counter is not yet a clear command center.

Production target:

- Register should read as the main work surface for checkout, returns, trade-ins, preorders, and services.
- Counter should have readable customer side and owner side.
- Required prop language: scanner, small register screen, card/cash area, receipt printer, bags or sleeves, impulse rack, queue marker, service/trade-in intake spot.
- Signage should be legible from normal player angles and never cropped into meaningless fragments.
- The register must remain interactable through center-reticle click with no extra terminal introduced.

### `customer_queue.png`

Current read:

- Role props help, but customers still look like placeholder capsules.
- Queue and special customers are separate enough for tests, but the scene does not yet sell store traffic.
- People feel placed for validation rather than living in the space.

Production target:

- Customer body kit should make roles readable before prompt text.
- Buyer queue should have a believable lane and customer-facing side of the counter.
- Special register customers should occupy a service/trade-in arc, not a crowd.
- Customers should imply intent through body shape, clothing color, carried item, posture, and animation state.
- The production view should distinguish buyer, trade-in seller, preorder/customer-service, and suspicious contact without relying only on color.

### `receiving_area.png`

Current read:

- Receiving, storage, and rack concepts exist, but signage is clipped and the area reads as stacked props.
- The receiving label is too large relative to the camera and obscures the state it is meant to clarify.
- Boxes and shelves do not yet communicate an organized backroom workflow.

Production target:

- Receiving should read as physical intake: delivery door or pallet, unopened boxes, open intake bin, invoice/packing slip, sorted stock lane, and overflow storage.
- Storage should read as backstock, not a second sales shelf.
- Rack and receiving affordances must stay separate: incoming product, stored product, and sellable product should be visually distinct.
- Optional supplier clue should be visible only when inspected or intentionally noticed, not as an objective marker.

### `backroom_summary.png`

Current read:

- Information architecture is improved, but the panel still feels like text over a scene.
- World geometry behind the translucent panel adds noise.
- Controls are grouped, but repeated-use ergonomics need a production UI style.

Production target:

- Computer UI should read as a task-based management OS: dashboard, inventory, ordering, releases, reports, storage, services, suppliers, and hidden records when discovered.
- Panel should use consistent typography, tabs, rows, buttons, disabled states, scroll behavior, and selected states.
- Important values should be visible at the decision point: cash, due day, cost, margin, obligation, inventory count, and consequence.
- World background should support context without fighting text readability.

### Held Item And Product Screenshots

Current read:

- Product cases validate as compact and readable, but they are still simple blocks with labels.
- Held stack stays out of the reticle, but it lacks hand/weight/settle feedback.
- Receiving and shelf scale is mechanically correct, not yet production appealing.

Production target:

- Used games should read as boxed/cased games at receiving, hand, shelf, customer, and register scale.
- Product variation should come from data-driven visual rules: platform band, condition wear, price tag, rarity marker, manual/complete cue, authenticity/risk cue.
- Held item presentation should suggest the player is carrying stock, not dragging a floating mesh.

### Fixture Placement Screenshots

Current read:

- Ghost, invalid, rotated, and placed states validate, but the visual language is prototype-like.
- Placement controls are tied to the backroom panel but not yet to a production building mode.

Production target:

- Fixture ghost should be transparent enough to read as preview and solid enough to judge footprint.
- Valid/invalid states must be readable under final store lighting.
- Placement UI should show cost, footprint, rotation, collision, pathing status, and confirm/cancel.
- Real placed fixtures must never look like ghosts.

## Art Direction Contract

### Style

Target style: stylized realism with readable retail exaggeration.

Rules:

- Shapes should be slightly simplified, but surfaces need enough bevels, details, labels, and wear to stop reading as raw primitives.
- Silhouettes matter more than texture detail.
- No hyperrealistic asset direction until the core loop is stable.
- No real brands, real products, real platform logos, or legally dependent trade dress.
- Fictional names should evoke late-90s/2000s game retail without copying existing retailers or consoles.

### Scale And Camera

Rules:

- Player camera should feel like a standing adult owner/operator at roughly eye level over normal counters.
- Counter height, shelf height, door height, and customer height must form a consistent human scale.
- The default player spawn should avoid ceiling-dominant framing.
- Props should be sized from interaction use: cases fit in hand and shelf slots, boxes fit on floor/pallets, monitors do not block work surfaces, signs fit readable wall space.
- Screenshot targets must preserve composition at `1280x720`.

### Palette

Starter production palette:

- Sales floor base: warm off-white walls, dark charcoal/graphite counters, muted warm gray floor, medium wood or laminate accents.
- Backroom base: cooler blue-gray walls, sealed concrete floor, utilitarian metal shelving, cardboard and paper tones.
- Brand accents: warm yellow signage, teal/green secondary accent, restrained red/orange only for warnings/deals.
- UI overlay: dark translucent panels with high-contrast text and a limited accent set.

Palette rules:

- Avoid a one-note palette dominated by a single hue.
- Keep dominant purple/purple-blue gradients, beige/cream/sand/tan, dark blue/slate, and brown/orange palettes out of the whole-screen read.
- Use accent color to guide tasks, not as decoration.
- Product and customer colors should remain readable under both sales-floor and backroom lighting.

### Lighting

Rules:

- Sales floor should feel warmer and more welcoming than the backroom.
- Backroom should feel practical and cooler, but still readable.
- Signage can glow subtly, but cannot bloom over text or prompts.
- Work surfaces need local light: register, trade-in spot, computer desk, receiving, and service bench.
- Prompt, reticle, price tags, and product labels must remain readable in the actual game window.

### Materials And Detail

Rules:

- Flat planes need material purpose: painted drywall, laminate counter, glass storefront, rubber mat, cardboard, metal shelving, plastic cases, paper signs.
- Edges should use bevels or trim where primitives currently meet abruptly.
- Clutter should indicate function: trade-in slips, preorder cards, sleeves, cases, shipping labels, repair tools, receipts, platform/category tags.
- Do not add clutter that can be mistaken for an interaction target unless it becomes one deliberately.

### Signage And Fictional Brand Language

Store brand:

- Primary store name remains `SAVE POINT GAMES` unless a later branding slice deliberately renames it.
- Primary tone: local specialty shop, not corporate megastore.

World signage rules:

- Signs should be short, readable, and physically plausible.
- Required starter signs: storefront/store name, register, trade-ins, used games, new releases/preorders, services, receiving, storage, staff/backroom.
- Signs must not clip, crop, or float through the player's normal view.
- Category labels should help stocking and browsing, not replace actual product visuals.

Fictional vocabulary examples:

- Categories: Used Games, New Releases, Trade-Ins, Retro, Controllers, Repairs, Preorders, Bargain Bin, Staff Picks.
- Services: Disc Resurfacing, Cartridge Cleaning, Controller Test, Console Check.
- Suppliers: North Pier Wholesale, Arcade Annex Distribution, Backlot Media Lots.
- Product/platform families should remain fictional and data-driven.

## Starter Store Layout Contract

The first production store should remain small. The goal is a believable one-owner shop, not a big expansion.

### Required Zones

Front/storefront:

- Front door with readable open/closed state.
- Storefront glass or interior-facing window cue.
- Store sign visible from entry/spawn.
- Hours/open sign or small door decal.

Sales floor:

- Primary used-game shelf or wall.
- New-release/preorder display.
- Bargain/impulse surface.
- Customer browsing lane.
- Clear path from entry to shelf to register.

Register:

- Customer side of counter.
- Owner side of counter.
- Register/scanner/payment/receipt prop set.
- Trade-in/service intake spot.
- Buyer queue point and special-customer arc.

Backroom:

- Receiving point.
- Storage/backstock shelves.
- Management computer desk.
- Service bench.
- Paperwork/invoice surface.
- Hidden-thread clue surfaces, subtle and optional.

### First View Composition

Default spawn should show:

- Store identity.
- Register command center.
- At least one product fixture.
- Customer route or queue hint.
- Backroom/receiving hint.

Default spawn should not show:

- Dominant empty ceiling.
- Cropped brand/sign fragments.
- Unlabeled gray props.
- All customers clustered without role separation.

### Navigation Rules

- Entry to shelf to register should be obvious.
- Register customer side and player work side should be visually distinct.
- Backroom access should feel like staff space, not another customer lane.
- Receiving and storage paths must support carrying multiple items.
- Fixture placement must not be able to block required paths.

### Starter Footprint

The starter footprint should support:

- One register counter.
- Two or three sellable product fixtures.
- One receiving area.
- One backroom storage wall.
- One computer desk.
- One service bench.
- Space for at least three customers without crowding.
- Space for one new fixture placement tutorial.

Do not expand the floor plan until this starter store reads well from screenshots and manual play.

### Implementation Implications

Future scene slices should prioritize:

- Reframing player spawn and camera composition before adding decorative props.
- Rebuilding counter and sign scale before adding more customer types.
- Separating receiving/storage/sellable rack visuals before adding more inventory categories.
- Establishing material and lighting rules before adding large content sets.
