# Art Direction Target

## Target

The store should feel like a small independent used video game shop in a small mall or retail strip around 2005-2007.

It should not feel like a sterile big-box retailer, a modern esports store, or a pure retro museum. It should feel operational: the owner is receiving used stock, pricing cases, handling trade-ins, managing preorders, and trying to make rent.

The opening composition currently targets a second-floor mall setting: quiet concourse, atrium railing, neighboring shuttered storefronts, planters, glass entry, neon trim, and a branded facade visible before the player enters.

## Style

Default style:

- Stylized semi-realistic indie sim.
- Authored low-poly/modular meshes with bevels and readable silhouettes.
- Texture and material detail through trim sheets, decals, posters, shelf strips, price stickers, and scuffed surfaces.
- Warm fluorescent sales floor, cooler backroom, and focused counter/shelf light.
- Fictional product/platform branding that evokes the era without real logos or trade dress.

Avoid:

- Pure graybox/CSG presentation.
- Giant text labels carrying object identity.
- Hyperreal asset scope that slows production.
- Modern LED gaming-room aesthetics.
- Meme/joke signage as the main tone.
- One-note color palettes.

## Era Markers

Use era cues carefully:

- DVD-style game cases and shelves.
- Used price stickers and trade-in stickers.
- Preorder cards and release-date posters.
- Strategy guides and gaming magazines.
- Memory cards, AV cables, wired controllers, cleaning kits.
- CRT/demo station or early LCD display.
- Beige/black office computer, landline phone, printer, invoice clipboard.
- Fluorescent ceiling fixtures, acoustic tiles, scuffed carpet/laminate, slatwall or pegboard.
- Mall-concourse tile, railing, shuttered neighbors, planter boxes, and glass storefront framing for the opening approach.

Do not use real console names, real publishers, real game titles, real retailer names, or recognizable logos.

## Mood

Surface mood:

- Warm.
- Local.
- Slightly cluttered.
- Practical.
- Familiar to someone who visited used game shops in the mid-00s.

Underlying mood:

- Thin margins.
- Awkward customers.
- Questionable trades.
- Weird invoices.
- Backroom stress.

The suspicious/hidden thread should feel like something odd inside normal retail work, not a horror-store overlay.

## Screenshot Bar

A screenshot passes the visual target only when:

- The store reads as a game shop before text is read.
- Materials, lighting, shelves, and product density sell the setting.
- The object role is clear by silhouette and placement.
- Labels support identity but do not carry it alone.
- The view feels authored, not assembled from debug markers.
- Navigation and interaction prompts remain readable.

If a screenshot still looks like a Godot editor blockout with labels, it fails visual production even if all tests pass.
