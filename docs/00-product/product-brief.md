# Product Brief

## Working Title

Game Store Sim

## Short Pitch

Open a small independent game store in an early-2000s mall and grow it through hands-on retail work: receive boxes, price used games, stock shelves, help customers, keep launch promises, read daily reports, and decide what kind of local shop people remember.

## Genre

First-person retail life sim.

## Primary Platform

macOS first.

Future platform expansion can be considered after the first playable proves the core loop.

## Audience

Primary audience:

- players who like tactile first-person job sims
- players who enjoy shop management, stocking, pricing, and layout games
- players nostalgic for physical game retail
- players who like slow-burn progression and store customization

Secondary audience:

- players interested in hidden systems and optional mystery layers
- players who enjoy optimizing retail flow and customer behavior
- players who like collecting fictional product catalogs

## Core Player Fantasy

The player is the owner-operator of a small mall game shop. They personally touch the business:

- carry stock from receiving
- put games on shelves
- choose prices
- arrange fixtures
- help customers
- close the register
- read reports
- plan tomorrow

The fantasy is not passive ownership. The player is physically present in the store.

## Tone

Warm nostalgia:

- mall concourse ambience
- fluorescent retail lighting
- early-2000s fixture language
- plastic game cases
- handwritten notes
- cardboard shipments
- simple price stickers
- customers who remember promises

The tone should be cozy and grounded, not parody-heavy. It can have humor, but the store should feel like a place worth caring about.

## What The Game Is

- a physical store sim
- a first-person job sim
- a long-form business growth game
- a layout and stocking game
- a pricing and reputation game
- a fictional early-2000s game retail fantasy
- eventually, a quiet mystery game embedded in normal retail work

## What The Game Is Not

- not a real-brand simulator
- not a real-chain clone
- not a giant-chain business sim
- not a supernatural mystery
- not a tycoon spreadsheet with a store skin
- not a pure decoration sandbox
- not a pixel-hunting secret game
- not a game where the first sale means meaningful completion

## IP And Brand Policy

All in-game commercial content is fictional:

- fictional game titles
- fictional platforms
- fictional accessories
- fictional publishers
- fictional distributors
- fictional store signage
- fictional product art
- fictional posters
- fictional customer references

Real inspiration may be used to extract visual and mechanical rules, but direct copying is forbidden.

## First Playable Product Promise

The first playable should make a player say:

> I can see how this becomes a real store sim.

It does not need to include every long-term system. It must prove that handling, pricing, stocking, opening, selling, closing, and saving feel good.

## Success Criteria

The first playable is successful when:

- the player understands the store by walking it
- physical stock handling feels reliable
- shelves communicate capacity and product presence
- customers feel physically present, not just menu entries
- the first sale feels earned
- the daily report creates a next-day decision
- save/load preserves the store accurately
- the Mac build runs cleanly
