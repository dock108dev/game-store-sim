## Decision policies and information boundary

The policy driver reads the same title interests and release dates shown by the day-1 Calendar, reference/supply prices, owned inventory, wage/rent/expansion disclosures and observed reports. It never reads future buyer budgets to choose prices, changes buyer definitions or rolls back a failed decision. State screening controls the state directly and bypasses walking; it is explicitly calculation evidence. The subsequent scene runs start with the ordinary New week button and complete all seven days with physical movement and ordinary controls. No synthetic day prelude, injected money, teleports, debug sales or changed rules occur in these runs.

- **Delegated growth:** buy the $60 rack on day 1; reference-price new stock; buy one C/T/O on days 3, 5 and 7, add two Signal on day 4 and two Signal/two Rally on day 6. These are calendar-based stock batches, not an exact-budget optimizer. Buy used offers on days 2–6 by offering $1 and accepting the displayed seller counter; label at the displayed condition reference on the following day. Refuse day 7. Hire Morgan on day 3 and Jules on day 6, stock in preparation then assign checkout. Move the stocked first rack 10px left on day 2; buy the $100 north bay on day 5. Both new releases, good/fair used resale, receiving, labeling, placing/moving racks, staffing in both roles, wages/rent, save/resume and the terminal report are exercised. The second rack is useful capacity growth (six-to-eight-copy preparation would exceed the original four slots). The north bay purchase opens the space and adds its rack, but this policy never exceeds eight displayed copies and leaves the third rack unused. Its extra capacity is not necessary for these sales; no investment payback is claimed. Meaningful utilized growth in this route is the second rack, not an asserted economic return from the north bay.

- **Lean discount shop:** buy the $60 rack, no expansion, no employees, no used acquisitions. Price new copies at a simple 10% discount to visible references from day 1; replenish only the current calendar demand shortfall after subtracting owned new stock. Stock and serve every copy manually. This is materially different procurement, pricing, trading, staffing and investment, rather than the growth purchases relabeled. The discount is a declared broad-price policy, not tailored to hidden individual budgets.

- **Pricing recovery:** same manual replenishment policy, but try 150% of reference on day 1. Three refusals and zero receipts lead to ordinary Reprice at reference on day 2. A further price refusal leads to the same broad 10% discount from day 3. Avoid buying duplicates of the unsold stock. All correction occurs in next preparation, preserving the original failed sales and paid expenses.

- **Separate bankruptcy:** buy the rack and six of each original title on both days 1 and 2, keep all stock off shelves, then employ both workers from day 3 without assigning useful work. Open and let the authored days finish. This is deliberate waste, not the recoverable ordinary mistake. No grants, balance overrides or bankruptcy injection.

- **Low-effort comparisons:** no selling or purchases; sell only the six prepaid copies with the original rack; growth without used acquisitions; and growth with Morgan only. The first three are calculation/state screens; one-worker growth is additionally a complete control-driven scene route.

## Tuning decision

**No game tuning.** Two materially different policies earn an operating profit, meaningful rack growth and the complete-system route are affordable, an ordinary pricing error recovers, and deliberate waste produces a correctly explained failure. Doing no retail work does not dominate profitable retail: no selling ends at $400 with a $150 operating loss; liquidating starter stock ends at $520 with an $80 operating loss. Those survivors consume starting assets. **For the binary survival label alone, doing nothing is a valid low-effort solution**: the starting cash exceeds rent. The intended loop offers profit, sales and shop development beyond that label; the report explicitly separates survival from profit. This first week therefore has weak mandatory financial pressure, and this evaluation does not establish that its motivation is compelling. Cutting starting cash merely to force trading would impose an unvalidated difficulty target rather than repair the demonstrated arithmetic or access requirements. B10 still needs to judge the clarity and appeal of those goals. The no-selling/minimal screens let all authored arrivals attempt purchases; closing immediately would replace those misses with canceled admissions but would not improve their cash or operating result. The no-used comparison gives up $35.40 operating profit, while saving five negotiations, five individual labels and five resales.

One worker produces the same sales as two, saving $24; the second worker performs stocking but is redundant at the single checkout. Lean manual operation keeps more cash, but requires every stock/service operation from the player. Optional labor and optional north-bay investment are convenience/space choices, not guaranteed profit multipliers. The generous starting cushion, lack of queue impatience and small authored week make staffing nonessential. These are explicit limits rather than grounds to force mandatory hiring, reduce starting capital or inflate demand. No evidence presently supports a particular price, cost or arrival/work-time adjustment without substituting a preferred cash result or untested owner preference. The observed walking snag is a functional clearance limitation, not evidence for a new price, wage or arrival interval; slowing demand to conceal it would not establish balance. The ordinary keyboard detour and failed attempt remain explicit. Consequently there is no old/new parameter table, changed game expectation, schema bump or save migration.
