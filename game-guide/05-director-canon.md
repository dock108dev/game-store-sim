# The Store Fantasy

Read this chapter before planning a serious run. It explains what Game Store Sim is really about.

## The Game

Game Store Sim is a first-person retail life sim about opening a small independent game store in the early 2000s and slowly turning it from an understocked startup into the kind of local shop people remember years later.

The game is not about becoming a giant chain. It is about operating one believable shop well:

- knowing what customers want
- choosing what to buy with limited cash
- pricing used goods without killing trust
- making launch-day promises carefully
- deciding which suppliers are worth trusting
- building a store that visually earns its growth
- noticing strange details without letting them consume the whole game

The ideal player feeling is:

> I opened this place myself. Every shelf tells me what I chose, what I missed, and what I am trying to become.

## Tone

The surface tone is warm, specific, and grounded:

- mall lights
- carpet
- cardboard boxes
- price stickers
- shelves with too much empty space at first
- customers who remember what you promised
- weird used games with handwritten notes
- launch posters that matter for exactly one weekend

The secret tone is quiet and plausible:

- questionable provenance
- off-manifest stock
- no-receipt cash offers
- serial mismatches
- collector rumors
- supplier pressure
- impossible timing

No supernatural lore. No world-ending conspiracy. The mystery feels like something that could happen in the margins of a used-game retail business.

## Core Loop

The loop is:

1. Prepare the store.
2. Open to customers.
3. Sell, advise, trade, service, and react.
4. Close the day.
5. Read what the business is telling you.
6. Order, reorganize, commit, or hold back.
7. Start the next day with consequences.

Everything routes through that loop.

## The Player's Real Job

The player is not just placing objects. The player is deciding what kind of shop this is.

Possible identities:

- careful neighborhood shop
- high-volume used-game trader
- launch-day specialist
- collector-friendly rarity shop
- service-and-repair trusted local
- bargain-bin treasure cave
- risky gray-market operator
- evidence-heavy clean operator

Hybrids work, but the store reflects your habits.

## Progression Pillars

Progression has seven axes:

1. Cash: can you afford the next risk?
2. Reputation: do customers believe your promises?
3. Supplier Trust: do better lots and cleaner terms open up?
4. Store Quality: does the shop look and function like a better retailer?
5. Catalog Access: do platforms, categories, and rare goods unlock?
6. Customer Depth: do more demanding archetypes trust the store?
7. Records Integrity: do your hidden-thread choices create clean, risky, or ambiguous history?

Cash is the loudest number but not the deepest progression. A rich store with poor reputation, bad suppliers, and suspicious records is unstable.

## Completion Philosophy

There is no single final boss. Completion means the store has matured.

The opening scene is tutorial-scale. Receiving starter stock, opening the store, making a first sale, and closing the first day are roughly 0.3% completion, not the first major chapter of the full game. The full scale is years of operating, expanding, adapting, and living with consequences.

Core 100%:

- stable daily operation
- mature store layout
- broad but legal-safe catalog
- trusted supplier network
- functional launch planning
- service/return/trade-in mastery
- visible store identity
- strong customer loyalty

Optional 100%:

- all customer archetypes resolved well
- all release-calendar patterns mastered
- all supplier types used
- all store zones built out
- all hidden clue surfaces discovered
- all major hidden routes resolved across replays

The player can keep running the shop after any ending.

## Secret Design Law

Secrets obey one rule:

> The player finds secrets by being a better shopkeeper, a riskier shopkeeper, or a more curious shopkeeper.

That means every secret unlock comes from a normal sim action:

- checking invoices
- pricing a strange trade-in
- comparing serials
- following up with a supplier
- keeping a promise to a collector
- refusing a no-receipt deal
- accepting a no-receipt deal
- isolating goods instead of selling them
- reviewing records after close

No hidden thread requires pixel hunting outside the retail loop.

## Canon Store Arc

The canonical store arc has five acts.

### Act I: Keys And Cardboard

You have a lease, a sign, a counter, a backroom, and too few goods. The store is closed. The first emotional hook is touching every piece of inventory yourself.

Main question: can this become a real store?

### Act II: First Customers

You open. Customers expose every weakness: bad prices, weak stock, confusing layout, missing launch planning.

Main question: can the business survive normal retail pressure?

### Act III: The Local Shop

Regulars appear. Collectors ask about release promises. Parents need advice. Trade-ins become the used wall's lifeblood. Services and returns make the store feel human.

Main question: what kind of shop are you becoming?

### Act IV: Access And Pressure

Better suppliers, rarer goods, launch days, and customer expectations scale together. Hidden-thread pressure also increases because more volume means more anomalies.

Main question: what are you willing to risk for growth?

### Act V: The Store People Remember

The store is full, but not generic. It has history: sold-out weekends, customer requests, odd records, trusted shelves, maybe a secret file of choices.

Main question: what did your store become, and what did it cost?

## Canon Ending Families

Core endings describe the business, not the end of play:

- Neighborhood Favorite: high trust, stable reputation, clean records.
- Launch-Day Machine: strong release planning and high new-release revenue.
- Used Wall Legend: strong trade-in loop, deep used catalog, collector interest.
- Service Staple: repair/service reputation drives repeat traffic.
- High-Risk Operator: strong cash, weak trust, high inspection risk.
- Balanced Local Icon: broad competence across all systems.

Secret endings layer on top of those, not instead of them.
