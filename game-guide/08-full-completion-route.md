# Full Completion Route

This is the canonical route for a balanced first 100% business playthrough. It corrects the scale: the opening scene and first sale are tutorial progress, not early-game completion.

## 0.0-0.3%: Tutorial Opening

Do:

1. Enter the store.
2. Find receiving.
3. Open the starter box.
4. Pick up and inspect starter stock.
5. Price one used item.
6. Stock one shelf.
7. Open the store.
8. Complete the first sale.
9. Close the day.
10. Read the daily summary.

Completion signal:

- you know how to touch the store's basic loop.

Do not treat this as meaningful completion. You have sold a product. You have not built a business.

## 0.3-1%: First Week Survival

Do:

1. Run several day loops.
2. Adjust at least one price after customer behavior.
3. Accept one trade-in.
4. Decline or negotiate one trade-in.
5. Place a starter supplier order.
6. Receive and sort the delivery.
7. Save and reload.

Completion signal:

- you can operate without tutorial framing.

Secret note:

- document early serial or supplier oddities, but do not chase the whole mystery yet.

## 1-3%: Basic Business Literacy

Do:

1. Complete normal sales.
2. Handle a return.
3. Complete one service job.
4. Use store credit once.
5. Use daily report reorder suggestions.
6. Keep the store from running out of sellable stock.

Completion signal:

- the store is fragile but functional.

## 3-5%: First Customer Patterns

Do:

1. Serve a browser.
2. Serve a parent/gift buyer.
3. Keep a regular happy.
4. Understand a target buyer's price sensitivity.
5. Keep the queue clear.

Completion signal:

- customer types are no longer generic traffic.

## 5-10%: Month-One Competence

Do:

1. Read the release calendar.
2. Commit allocation for one launch.
3. Take one preorder.
4. Fulfill or fail launch-day demand.
5. Learn from reputation change.
6. Improve a fixture or store zone.

Completion signal:

- the store has a future-facing planning loop.

## 10-20%: Store Identity Begins

Do:

1. Decide the store's early identity.
2. Deepen the used wall.
3. Add accessories or guide/media support.
4. Improve bargain/new-release/platform zones.
5. Use at least two supplier lot types.
6. Keep services and returns from damaging trust.

Completion signal:

- the store is becoming a specific kind of shop.

## 20-35%: Reliable Local Shop

Do:

1. Complete multiple release cycles.
2. Build customer loyalty.
3. Expand catalog access.
4. Upgrade fixtures for function, not decoration.
5. Keep backstock organized.
6. Maintain positive cash without gutting trust.

Completion signal:

- regulars and collectors have reasons to come back.

Secret note:

- choose a hidden-thread stance: clean records, cautious archive, risky profit, supplier investigation, or pure retail.

## 35-50%: Mature First-Era Store

Do:

1. Fill the first-era used wall with meaningful depth.
2. Run launch days confidently.
3. Maintain service reputation.
4. Keep returns under control.
5. Use multiple supplier relationships intentionally.
6. Build out demo, bargain, guide/media, hardware, and platform areas.

Completion signal:

- the 2002-2004 shop is no longer a startup. It is a mature local store.

## 50-65%: Era Transition

Do:

1. Introduce new platform pressure.
2. Move older stock into legacy/used positioning.
3. Rework layout without breaking customer flow.
4. Decide which supplier relationships still fit.
5. Use store quality to support larger catalog breadth.

Completion signal:

- success has created complexity instead of ending the game.

## 65-80%: Destination Shop

Do:

1. Build rare/collector inventory paths.
2. Negotiate or earn better allocation access.
3. Use premium fixtures for high-value stock.
4. Keep the store dense but readable.
5. Protect reputation through promises, services, and fair handling.

Completion signal:

- customers come because of the store itself.

## 80-95%: Legacy Layer

Do:

1. Resolve long-running supplier consequences.
2. Complete major customer loyalty arcs.
3. Establish final store identity.
4. Review hidden records and decide what remains unresolved.
5. Finish mature catalog and layout goals.

Completion signal:

- the store has history, not just progress.

## 95-100%: Completion

Do:

1. Stabilize cash, reputation, supplier trust, store quality, catalog access, customer loyalty, and records integrity.
2. Finish the business ending family.
3. Resolve one secret ending family or intentionally keep the thread unresolved.
4. Continue operating after the ending summary.

Completion signal:

- the store has become a local institution with a player-authored identity.

## Balanced First 100% Ending

Aim for:

- high reputation
- healthy cash
- medium-high supplier trust
- high store quality
- broad catalog access
- high customer loyalty
- high records integrity
- low inspection risk

Likely combined ending:

- business ending: Balanced Local Icon
- secret ending: Clean Records or Backroom Archivist

## Replay Routes For True Secret 100%

True secret completion takes multiple stores.

### Risk Route

Accept suspicious cash, sell some anomalies, use risky suppliers, and trigger Cash Habit or Audit outcomes.

### Collector Route

Fulfill launches, protect holds, build rare trust, and trigger Collector Confidence.

### Supplier Investigator

Read every invoice, follow up supplier threads, reject suspicious goods, and trigger Supplier Reckoning.

### Pure Retail

Ignore records anomalies, refuse suspicious contacts, and prove the sim works without mystery escalation.

### Archive Route

Document, isolate, and preserve evidence without always reporting. Trigger Backroom Archivist.
