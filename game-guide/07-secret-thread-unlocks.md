# Secret Web Unlocks

Game Store Sim has one secret web, not a pile of separate questlines. It begins quietly, branches through normal retail choices, resolves through one of several outcomes, then drops you back into the store. The business continues.

You can miss the web completely. That is a valid playthrough.

## The Web Structure

The secret web has five phases.

1. Noise: odd details appear but do not matter yet.
2. Pattern: repeated details connect suppliers, products, customers, or records.
3. Pressure: someone asks you to choose money, silence, evidence, loyalty, or risk.
4. Breakpoint: the web resolves through a major event.
5. Reset: the event is summarized, consequences are applied lightly, and the store continues.

The secret web never ends the business. Even the harshest outcomes become a completion/information screen, then the store reopens.

## When It Starts

The web stays quiet early. The first sale, first week, and first basic supplier loops are about learning the store.

Real secret pressure starts after the store has enough activity to make anomalies meaningful:

- repeated receiving batches
- several trade-ins, if you accept trade-ins
- launch-day activity
- supplier history
- backroom records
- enough regular customer traffic for rumors to matter

Players who ignore odd details can run a normal store indefinitely.

## Core Clues

The web uses normal business objects:

- mismatched serial numbers
- invoice count errors
- supplier notes
- no-receipt cash offers
- impossible release timing
- questionable used goods
- repeated returns from one source
- backroom security notes
- employee handling records, if your store has staff

None of these force a quest marker. They become meaningful when patterns repeat.

## Major Choice Stances

You do not choose a named route. Your habits choose it for you.

| Stance | How You Play | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oblivious | Ignore records and odd details. | The web may pass by without resolution. |
| Clean Operator | Document, isolate, reject, and report. | Lower risk, possible supplier loss. |
| Profit First | Sell questionable goods and take cash. | Higher cash, higher event risk. |
| Quiet Fixer | Keep evidence, handle issues privately. | Ambiguous results, fewer clean answers. |
| Supplier Loyalist | Protect supplier access and avoid reports. | Better stock, worse records. |
| Staff Shield | Let employees handle risky counter/backroom work. | Staff can become the fall point. |

## Breakpoint Outcomes

### Nothing Happens

The player ignores enough details, or the store never creates enough pattern pressure.

Result:

- no dramatic event
- store continues normally
- records remain thin
- replay can reveal what was missed

### Clean Closure

The player documents suspicious goods, isolates risk, and reports only once the pattern is clear.

Result:

- reputation improves
- inspection risk drops
- one supplier path may close
- cleaner supplier access can open later
- store continues the next business day

### Supplier Fallout

The player follows supplier clues but protects the store from direct risk.

Result:

- supplier access changes
- some lots disappear
- replacement suppliers appear
- customers barely notice unless stock runs thin
- store continues

### Robbery

The player accepts risky cash, stores questionable high-value goods, or leaves backroom security weak.

Result:

- a robbery event triggers
- cash or inventory is lost
- security upgrades become more valuable
- reputation may dip if customer goods were involved
- store reopens after the event summary

### Inspection

The player sells suspicious goods as normal too often.

Result:

- inspection screen summarizes questionable records
- cash, reputation, and supplier trust may shift
- some stock is removed or quarantined
- store continues

### You Get Arrested

The player repeatedly chooses risky cash, off-record sales, and bad paperwork while keeping responsibility centered on the owner.

Result:

- arrest/court summary appears
- store progress is not wiped
- the sim resumes after the event with penalties, reputation damage, or temporary restrictions
- this is a secret-web ending, not a game over

### Employee Gets Arrested

If the store has employees and the player routes risky handling through staff, the employee can become the fall point.

Result:

- staff member is removed
- trust and morale suffer
- records integrity is damaged
- the store continues

### Testify In Court

The player documents enough evidence and follows the formal route.

Result:

- court/testimony summary appears
- supplier or customer web resolves
- reputation can improve
- supplier access may change
- store continues

### Quiet Compromise

The player keeps the store safe enough but never fully resolves the pattern.

Result:

- no arrest, no clean closure
- some supplier tension remains
- secret records persist
- store continues with ambiguity

## Reset Rule

After any breakpoint:

1. Show the outcome summary.
2. Apply light consequences.
3. Clear or archive the active secret web.
4. Return to the store.
5. Let normal business continue.
6. Allow a later secret variation to seed again.

The secret web adds surprise and replay value. It does not delete the sim.

## Best First Run Strategy

For a first serious run:

1. Ignore tiny oddities until the business is stable.
2. Document repeated suspicious patterns.
3. Isolate goods instead of selling them.
4. Refuse early no-receipt cash.
5. Report only after multiple clues connect.

This usually leads to Clean Closure, Supplier Fallout, or Quiet Compromise.

## Best Replay Strategies

- Oblivious run: never open records unless required.
- Risk run: take cash, sell through, ignore paperwork.
- Staff shield run: delegate risky work once employees exist.
- Court run: document everything and report cleanly.
- Robbery run: hold risky high-value goods without enough security.
- Supplier run: follow invoice and delivery patterns instead of customer clues.
