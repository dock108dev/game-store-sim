# Core Systems

This chapter explains the systems you must master from your first box of stock to a fully mature local game shop.

## Controls And View

Game Store Sim is played from a first-person view with keyboard and mouse. Aim at the object you want, then interact. When a menu opens, the mouse becomes a management tool; when you close it, you return to floor control.

The core verbs are:

- walk the store
- inspect
- pick up
- carry
- price
- stock
- sell
- trade in
- order
- receive
- review reports
- save/load

## Store Areas

Your first store contains the major functions every successful shop depends on. You choose where they live.

- entrance/storefront
- new releases, if you stock them
- used games, if you accept trade-ins or buy used lots
- platform/category organization arranged however you prefer
- checkout counter
- trade-in station at checkout, if you run trade-ins
- demo area, if you invest in it
- bargain bin, if your store uses discount stock
- guides/media, if you stock them
- hardware/accessories
- receiving/backroom
- office/management desk

Learn the store by walking it. If you know where receiving, checkout, active stock, backstock, and customer traffic live, you know how the business flows. There is no single mandatory layout.

## Store Day Clock

Prep time is unlimited. Before you open, take as long as you want to receive boxes, move shelves, adjust prices, change signs, repaint, lay carpet, place displays, or reorganize the entire shop.

Open hours are timed. A normal store day runs around 15-20 minutes of real time. Friday and Saturday run longer because traffic stays heavier. Sunday runs shorter and rewards efficient prep.

When the open-hours timer ends:

- no new customers enter
- customers already inside keep browsing, buying, asking questions, or leaving
- active trade-ins, returns, services, and checkout lines continue
- you can keep working after the final customer leaves
- the day advances only when you shut the register

Closing is your choice. The clock stops new traffic, not your control of the store.

## Inventory

Every item has business meaning. Pay attention to:

- product identity
- unique instance ID
- price
- cost basis
- market value
- condition
- platform/category
- serial metadata
- location

Early inventory is limited. Unavailable products, distributors, platforms, and opportunities are not missing content; they are future goals.

## Receiving

Receiving begins in the backroom, not on the sales floor.

The receiving flow:

1. A shipment arrives.
2. The box or batch appears in receiving.
3. The player opens the box.
4. The invoice can be checked.
5. Items are sorted.
6. Goods move to backstock, shelves, counter, or review.
7. The receiving batch is completed.

Receiving supports ordinary retail work and optional secrets. Suspicious invoices, supplier notes, serial mismatches, and provenance gaps all begin as backroom details.

## Carrying And Stocking

The player can pick up products and place them onto valid shelf/rack slots.

Fixtures have readable capacity states:

- empty
- partially stocked
- stocked
- full

An understocked shelf is not always bad. Empty capacity shows where growth belongs.

## Pricing

Pricing happens while handling products. Use it before placing used goods on the floor.

The pricing readout shows:

- cost
- market value
- current price
- suggested range
- margin
- demand
- warnings for below-cost, below-range, above-range, and batch-pricing decisions

The player can price one item or apply a used-item price to matching copies.

## Customers

Customers validate the layout and business.

| Archetype | Role | What They Teach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browser | Browses active shelves/accessories | Product quality and fair pricing. |
| Target Buyer | Wants a specific item | Duplicate pricing and shelf matching. |
| Parent Gift Buyer | Needs help | Recommendations and gift-safe stock. |
| Launch Regular | Launch/preorder interest | Allocation and reputation. |
| Regular | Repeat trust | Promise keeping and discoveries. |
| Trade-In Seller | Brings goods | Offer quality and used-stock growth. |
| Return Customer | Brings a complaint | Reputation protection. |
| Service Customer | Needs repair/cleaning | Service bench workflow. |
| Suspicious Contact | Optional secret-web probe | Nonblocking mystery route. |

Normal customers queue and shop. Suspicious contacts stay outside the normal sales lane unless you choose to engage them.

## Register

The register/counter is one shared station for:

- sales
- returns
- trade-ins
- preorder deposits
- service pickup/completion

The counter is the store's pressure point. Sales, returns, trade-ins, preorders, and service pickups all compete for your attention there.

## Trade-Ins

Trade-ins can be a major business driver, but they are optional. A clean new-games-only shop can refuse trade-ins entirely and build around launch planning, accessories, and services instead.

The trade-in flow:

1. Seller brings an item.
2. The store evaluates condition/completeness/risk.
3. The player reviews cash and store-credit options.
4. Accepted goods move into receiving or review.
5. Cash trade-ins reduce cash.
6. Store-credit trade-ins create credit value without immediate cash spend.
7. Good inventory becomes used stock.

Trade-ins are also a key optional mystery channel because anomalous goods can enter the store here.

## Returns

Returns protect customer trust. A return customer may bring a defective copy and receipt. You can exchange, offer a service check, or refuse.

Handle returns at the counter. The backroom is for management and receiving, not customer arguments.

## Services

Services add a steady trust-and-revenue line.

Common service jobs include:

- disc resurfacing
- cartridge cleaning
- controller testing

The service loop:

1. Customer requests service.
2. Backroom or service bench creates/works the ticket.
3. Register completes pickup/payment.
4. Ledger records revenue, cost, and profit.
5. Daily report summarizes service work.

## Supplier Ordering

Suppliers are how the store grows.

Common supplier lots include:

- Used Game Starter Lot from North Dock Wholesale.
- New Release Sampler Lot from Clear Cart Distribution.
- Accessory Counter Lot from Shelf Pin Supply.
- Backstock Used Depth Lot from Harbor Bin Exchange.

Supplier orders reserve cash, create pending deliveries, and eventually generate receiving batches. Supplier trust matters as much as money. You cannot buy your way into the whole catalog on day one.

## Store Expansion

The first store is only the first footprint.

As profit, reputation, and rent history grow, neighboring mall units become available. You can buy out or lease adjacent spaces, then break through walls to expand the shop. Expansions create real layout choices:

- widen the sales floor
- add a dedicated service room
- build a larger receiving/backstock zone
- create a launch-event area
- add a demo lounge
- build a premium display room
- keep the original shop compact and use the next unit mostly for storage

Expansion is not just more square footage. Bigger stores need better signage, clearer paths, more fixtures, stronger stocking discipline, and enough traffic to justify the rent.

## Release Calendar And Launch Allocation

Upcoming releases are planned through the backroom.

The player can:

- view releases sorted by launch day
- read demand hooks and allocation notes
- commit allocation
- reserve cash
- take preorder deposits
- resolve launch-day preorder and walk-in demand

Shortages reduce reputation. Extra launch stock can satisfy queue demand.

## Daily Summary And Backroom Computer

The backroom computer is the management center.

Use it for:

- dashboard
- activity
- inventory
- ordering
- releases
- reports
- services
- storage
- suppliers
- settings
- records

The daily summary turns play into decisions: what sold, what sat, what to reorder, which releases need allocation, which services are ready, and what tomorrow requires.

## Save And Persistence

Save slots preserve your store's history:

- session state
- transactions
- active inventory
- save-slot metadata
- supplier orders
- receiving batches
- preorder deposits
- launch events
- reputation
- services
- management desk history
- secret-web choices and consequences

Save before major orders, launch commitments, and risky secret choices.
