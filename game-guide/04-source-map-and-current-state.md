# Quick Reference

Use this section when you need a fast answer during play.

## Completion Scale

| Progress | What It Means |
| ---: | --- |
| 0.0% | Empty lease. You have a store, not a business. |
| 0.1% | Starter shipment touched and first stock placed. |
| 0.3% | First sale and first close complete. Tutorial is over. |
| 1% | First week survived. Trade-ins and supplier basics are active. |
| 5% | Customer patterns and early reputation are visible. |
| 10% | Month-one shop: launch calendar and store identity begin. |
| 25% | Reliable local store with multiple systems feeding each other. |
| 50% | Mature first-era shop. You understand the 2002-2004 business. |
| 75% | Destination shop with deep catalog, loyalty, and rare demand. |
| 100% | Complete store identity plus resolved or preserved secret history. |

## Daily Priority Order

1. Check cash and pending orders.
2. Review release calendar and preorders.
3. Receive and sort deliveries.
4. Price used stock.
5. Stock visible shelves before backstock.
6. Open the store.
7. Serve customers by urgency.
8. Close only after the floor is clear.
9. Read the daily report.
10. Plan tomorrow before advancing.

## Queue Priority

When several customers need the counter:

1. Waiting buyer with product in hand.
2. Return customer who is blocking trust.
3. Service pickup ready to complete.
4. Preorder customer before launch pressure rises.
5. Trade-in seller when you have cash and receiving space.
6. Suspicious contact only if you deliberately want the risk.

## Early Cash Rules

- Keep enough cash for at least one reorder.
- Do not spend all cash on fixtures before building stock flow.
- Use store credit when a trade-in is good but cash is tight.
- Hold launch allocation money before decorating.
- A sold-out shelf is only good if tomorrow's receiving plan exists.

## Reputation Rules

Reputation rises when you:

- price fairly
- fulfill launch promises
- handle returns calmly
- complete services
- recommend correctly
- keep collector holds
- document or isolate suspicious goods

Reputation falls when you:

- overprice obvious stock
- miss promised launch copies
- refuse legitimate returns
- sell questionable goods as normal
- break collector trust
- let risky cash deals pile up

## Supplier Rules

Safe suppliers are steady but rarely exciting.

Risky suppliers are tempting but create paperwork, provenance, and inspection pressure.

Prestige suppliers reward strong reputation and launch performance. Do not waste their trust on careless allocation.

## Secret Thread Rules

- Secrets are optional.
- Clean records reduce long-term risk.
- Suspicious cash solves short-term problems and creates long-term ones.
- Isolating goods buys time.
- Reporting protects trust but can close supplier doors.
- Selling anomalies as normal is the fastest risky route.
- One save cannot reveal every secret ending.

## Best First 100% Route

For a balanced first completion:

1. Play clean through the first week.
2. Build the used wall before over-expanding fixtures.
3. Learn release allocation by taking one controlled risk.
4. Document suspicious items instead of selling them.
5. Build collector trust.
6. Use supplier follow-ups sparingly.
7. Finish as a Balanced Local Icon or Clean Records shop.
