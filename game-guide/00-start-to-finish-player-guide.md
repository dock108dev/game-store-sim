# Start-To-Finish Player Guide

This is the 0% to 100% player journey for Game Store Sim. The opening scene, tutorial, first sale, and first close are not 10-20% completion. They are the first fraction of a percent: you have learned how the store breathes, but have barely begun the business.

## Scale Rule

Completion is measured by store maturity, not by tutorial milestones.

Opening the store and making a sale is about 0.3% completion.

The full game spans years of store operation:

- opening setup
- first sales
- supplier access
- used wall growth
- launch calendar mastery
- customer loyalty
- services and returns
- fixture and layout evolution
- catalog expansion
- store identity
- era transitions
- rare inventory
- high-trust community status
- optional secret-thread routes across multiple replays

## 0.0%: Empty Lease

You begin with a lease, a sign, a counter, a small receiving area, a backroom, limited cash, and almost no inventory.

The store is not a complete game store yet. It feels like the morning before the first real day: clean, underfunded, lightly staged, and full of possibility.

Read the space this way:

- this is a small independent mall game shop
- the owner is doing the work personally
- the store is not fully stocked
- the backroom matters
- growth will be visible

## 0.1%: Opening Setup

The store is closed. No customers are present.

This is the tutorial, not the first act of the full game.

You learn:

1. Enter the store from the mall/concourse.
2. Find the receiving area.
3. Inspect the starter shipment.
4. Pick up a product.
5. Price a used item.
6. Stock a shelf/rack.
7. Find the counter.
8. Open the backroom computer.
9. Save.

This sequence teaches the tactile language of the sim. You touch the business before the business touches you.

## 0.3%: First Sale

You open the store and complete the first sale.

This is the moment the store becomes real, but it is still tutorial-scale progress.

You have only proven:

- customers can enter
- a product can be bought
- the register works
- the ledger records activity
- the daily loop can close

They have not built a store identity, supplier network, customer reputation, catalog, or long-term progression.

## 0.5%: First Close

The first daily summary teaches that the game is not just about selling one item.

The report introduces:

- cash
- revenue
- cost
- margin
- inventory remaining
- reorder suggestions
- tomorrow planning

Every day creates the next day.

## 1%: First Week Survival

The first real milestone is surviving several days without running the store into a dead end.

By this point, you have:

- made several normal sales
- accepted or declined at least one trade-in
- adjusted pricing after customer behavior
- placed a small supplier order
- received a delivery
- completed at least one close/report/next-day cycle

At 1%, the store is still fragile.

## 3%: Working Store

The store now has basic rhythm.

You understand:

- receiving
- pricing
- stocking
- selling
- trade-ins
- returns
- services
- supplier orders
- daily reports
- save/load

The shop still looks sparse. That is correct. This is not the mature-store fantasy yet.

## 5%: First Local Pattern

Customers begin to reveal what kind of shop the player is running.

Examples:

- browsers respond to fair used prices
- parents need recommendations
- collectors care about release promises
- regulars notice odd stock
- trade-in sellers test offer discipline
- service customers test follow-through

At this point, the player can no longer treat each day as isolated. Reputation begins to matter.

## 10%: Stable Month-One Business

The store has survived the novelty period.

Expected state:

- used wall has meaningful stock
- new-release planning has begun
- backroom computer is part of daily routine
- supplier lots are understood
- the player has seen enough customers to identify patterns
- at least one launch allocation decision has happened

This is the first point where the player may feel, "I have a business."

## 15%: First Store Identity

The store starts developing a personality.

Possible identities:

- used-game bargain shop
- launch-day specialist
- collector-friendly counter
- service-and-repair local
- family recommendation shop
- high-risk cash-heavy operator

The layout, stock, pricing, and customer history start reflecting your habits.

## 25%: Reliable Local Shop

The store has a reliable daily loop and recognizable local role.

Expected state:

- multiple supplier sources
- platform sections becoming legible
- trade-ins feeding used inventory
- services and returns integrated
- launch promises handled with planning
- store fixtures improved beyond starter setup
- customers repeat based on trust or disappointment

The store is still one shop, but it is no longer just surviving.

## 35%: Catalog Expansion

The player now manages breadth.

Unlock pressure comes from:

- supplier trust
- reputation
- store quality
- launch performance
- customer demand
- era timing

Money alone does not unlock everything.

At this stage, you are making real tradeoffs:

- buy more common used stock or reserve cash for launch allocation
- expand accessories or deepen new releases
- build a bargain bin or invest in premium display
- keep questionable goods isolated or sell through for cash

## 50%: Mature First-Era Store

The 2002-2004 version of the store is now mature.

Expected state:

- strong used wall
- credible new-release area
- platform sections for major fictional platforms
- trade-in counter is a major business driver
- demo/guides/media/bargain support exists
- backroom receiving and office work are routine
- regulars and collectors have history with the shop
- launch days are planned rather than improvised

This is the halfway point because the first era is mastered, not because the whole game is almost done.

## 65%: Transition Pressure

The business now faces change.

New platforms, customer expectations, supplier terms, and store density create pressure:

- old stock becomes legacy inventory
- newer platforms demand display space
- collectors want cleaner holds
- suppliers offer bigger but riskier lots
- services can become a reputation pillar
- store layout must adapt without losing identity

Success has created complexity.

## 75%: Destination Store

The store is now a place people intentionally visit.

Expected state:

- dense but navigable shop
- recognizable product and platform identity
- strong customer loyalty
- release calendar competence
- deep used inventory
- meaningful fixture and display choices
- better supplier access
- rare/collector demand

At 75%, the store is no longer just a local business. It is a destination within its niche.

## 85%: Store Legacy

The store has history.

The world remembers:

- missed or fulfilled launches
- trusted or broken collector promises
- suspicious stock decisions
- supplier relationships
- service reputation
- pricing habits
- long-running customer preferences

This stage is less about unlocking basic systems and more about living with the store's accumulated identity.

## 95%: Final Mastery

You have mastered the sim's major business layers:

- daily operations
- inventory flow
- supplier strategy
- launch planning
- trade-in economics
- services and returns
- store layout
- catalog breadth
- customer loyalty
- reputation management
- hidden-thread stance

The last 5% is not more tutorial. It is polish, completion, rare routes, and replay closure.

## 100%: Complete Store, Complete Read

100% means the player has built a store that can stand on its own.

Core 100%:

- stable multi-era store operation
- mature, credible store layout
- broad catalog access
- strong customer identity
- trusted or intentionally risky supplier network
- launch-day competence
- trade-ins/services/returns fully integrated
- visible growth from empty lease to destination shop

Secret/replay 100%:

- major secret threads resolved across multiple playthroughs
- clean, risky, supplier, collector, isolation, and pure-retail routes explored
- hidden outcomes layered on top of business endings
- store operation continues after any route

The final achievement is not "you sold a lot." It is "this shop has a history, a reputation, and a personality that came from the way you ran it."
