# Premium Strategy Notes

These are high-value tactics that separate a surviving shop from a legendary one.

## The First Sale Is Not Progress

The first sale is proof that the register works. It is not proof that your store works. Do not judge the shop by opening-day cash. Judge it by whether tomorrow has stock, clean shelves, and a reason for customers to return.

## Empty Space Is A Tool

Early empty shelf capacity is not failure. It shows the player where growth will happen. Fill the store too quickly with low-quality goods and you lose one of the strongest feelings in the game: watching the shop become real.

## Never Let Cash Lie To You

A cash-heavy day can still be a bad day if it came from:

- risky no-receipt sales
- underpriced rare goods
- missed preorder commitments
- stock you cannot replace
- trade-ins you did not inspect

Read reputation, supplier trust, and records integrity alongside cash.

## Your Main Category Is Your Heartbeat

New releases create spikes. Used games create rhythm. Services build trust. Accessories add margin. Your store's heartbeat is whichever category your layout and buying habits support best.

A strong stocked category:

- gives browsers something to do
- keeps regulars checking in
- feeds bargain-bin decisions
- gives trade-ins visible purpose
- creates rare-game surprises
- makes the store feel local

If the store feels dead, fix your most important stocked category first. That may be used games, new releases, accessories, services, or bargain goods depending on your build.

## Launch Days Are Reputation Tests

Do not chase every launch. Pick the releases you can support.

Strong launch play:

1. Read demand tier.
2. Reserve cash early.
3. Count preorder promises.
4. Keep launch stock visible.
5. Leave room for walk-ins only after preorder coverage.

Shortages hurt most when you promised too much.

## Trade-Ins Are Negotiations, Not Purchases

A trade-in is three decisions at once:

- Do I want this item?
- Can I afford this offer?
- What does this choice say about the store?

Cash offers protect margin. Store credit protects cash. Declining protects discipline.

## Services Build Trust Quietly

Services do not look as dramatic as launch days, but they build repeat traffic. A customer who gets a disc resurfaced, a controller tested, or a cartridge cleaned has a reason to trust the counter again.

## Suspicious Cash Is A Story Lever

Taking suspicious cash is not a mistake if you are playing that route. It is a commitment. The guide's advice: accept it only when you want the store's records to remember that kind of owner.

## Best Beginner Secret Strategy

For a first run:

1. Document everything.
2. Isolate suspicious goods.
3. Refuse the first cash offer.
4. Follow up with suppliers only when you have multiple clues.
5. Report only when the pattern is clear.

This route keeps options open and avoids locking into the riskiest endings too early.

## Best Replay Secret Strategy

For later runs, specialize:

- Profit-First run: accept every suspicious cash offer.
- Quiet Fixer run: document and isolate but rarely report.
- Supplier Investigator run: chase invoices and supplier follow-ups.
- Staff Shield run: let employees handle questionable goods after hiring.
- Robbery run: let high-value risk build while underinvesting in security.
- Pure Retail run: ignore secrets and prove the shop can thrive without them.

## Final Advice

The best store is not the richest store. The best store is the one where every area tells the same story: what you sell, who trusts you, what you risked, and what you refused.
