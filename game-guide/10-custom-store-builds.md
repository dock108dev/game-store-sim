# Custom Store Builds

No two successful stores need to look the same. Game Store Sim rewards stores that make sense, not stores that copy a fixed layout.

## Build Philosophy

Every store has the same business pressures:

- customers need clear paths
- products need visible homes
- receiving needs enough space
- the counter must stay readable
- backstock must not swallow the sales floor
- signage must help without doing all the work

How you solve those pressures is up to you.

## Product-Mix Builds

### New-Only Launch Shop

Focus on new games, preorder deposits, launch allocation, clean displays, front-facing boxes, and launch posters.

Skip or minimize trade-ins, bargain bins, and risky used lots.

Strength: clean reputation and strong launch identity.

Weakness: high cash pressure and missed-launch risk.

### Used Game Cave

Focus on trade-ins, used lots, dense shelving, price discipline, and rare finds.

Skip or minimize heavy new-release allocation and expensive launch displays.

Strength: steady browsing and strong margin opportunities.

Weakness: more inspection, condition, and provenance pressure.

### Service Counter Local

Focus on disc resurfacing, cartridge cleaning, controller testing, trusted counter flow, and a repair/service room after expansion.

Strength: strong customer trust and repeat visits.

Weakness: less dramatic sales spikes than launch-heavy stores.

### Family Recommendation Shop

Focus on clear shelf signs, gift-safe recommendations, broad mid-price stock, helpful counter placement, and clean returns.

Strength: reliable reputation.

Weakness: fewer rare/high-margin swings.

### Risky Backroom Operator

Focus on cheap supplier lots, cash offers, fast turnover, and isolated goods only when necessary.

Strength: fast cash.

Weakness: inspection risk, trust loss, and secret-web consequences.

## Layout Builds

### Straight Shot

Counter visible from entrance, shelves along the sides, clear backroom path.

Best for small stores, new players, and high register volume.

### Browse Loop

Customers enter, loop through displays, and reach the counter last.

Best for used-heavy stores, bargain-bin stores, and accessory attach sales.

### Split Shop

One side handles new/launch goods, the other handles used/service/bargain.

Best for expanded stores and mixed product identities.

### Backroom-Forward

Receiving and service sit closer to the counter, with sales floor arranged around operational speed.

Best for trade-in stores, service shops, and high-volume receiving.

### Boutique Compact

Small footprint, high-quality fixtures, controlled stock, clean sightlines.

Best for no-expansion runs, new-only stores, and rare-game specialists.

## Expansion Builds

### Wall Blowout Sales Floor

Buy the neighboring unit, open the wall, and turn both spaces into one large sales floor.

Best for high catalog breadth, platform/category browsing, and destination-shop builds.

### Service Annex

Use the neighboring unit for repair, testing, cleaning, and pickup flow.

Best for service-counter locals and trust-heavy runs.

### Backstock Annex

Keep the customer-facing shop compact and use the new space for storage, receiving, and sorting.

Best for used-heavy stores, supplier-heavy stores, and players who hate cluttered sales floors.

### Launch Event Room

Use expansion space for release displays, preorder pickup, demo stations, and weekend launch traffic.

Best for new-only launch shops and high-reputation launch routes.

## Fixture Choices

Use fixtures to express the store's identity:

- wall shelves: efficient product density
- gondolas: browsing loops and platform/category aisles
- bargain bins: treasure-hunting energy
- glass cases: high-value trust and rare goods
- guide/media racks: era authenticity
- demo kiosks: customer dwell and launch excitement
- service bench: repair identity
- counter impulse racks: accessory attach sales

## Visual Customization

Customize the store without losing readability:

- wall color
- carpet color and pattern
- tile/carpet transitions
- ceiling lights
- accent lights
- storefront sign
- open/closed sign
- shelf labels
- display headers
- posters
- sale tags
- price stickers
- counter materials
- fixture finishes
- figurines and display props
- demo screens
- plants, benches, and mall-facing details

The best visual rule: products create the color, fixtures create the structure, signage creates the final read.

## Common Build Mistakes

- buying too much floor space before stock flow can support it
- hiding the counter behind fixtures
- making receiving too far from backstock
- filling every wall with posters instead of products
- using signs to fix a confusing layout
- accepting trade-ins without enough review space
- ordering new releases without a visible launch home
- expanding because you can, not because the store needs it
